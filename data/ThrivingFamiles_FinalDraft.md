**PILLAR: THRIVING FAMILIES\
Workshop Discussion Themes**

**1. Youth employment should extend beyond summer jobs**

Participants noted that the framing around summer employment was too narrow. The opportunity is broader: helping teens access multiple forms of early work exposure. The revised framing expands from summer jobs to youth employment pathways.

**2. Youth need preparation and guidance, not just job listings**

The challenge is not only discovering opportunities but helping youth prepare to participate. Technology could help create a guided pathway from interest → readiness → employment.

**3. Employers also face friction hiring youth**

Workshop notes highlighted that employers are part of the system challenge. Businesses often lack confidence hiring teens, clarity on youth labor requirements, and systems to manage youth hiring. Participants suggested solutions could include verification or badges for youth-ready employers or mechanisms to connect employers with prepared youth.

**4. Youth employment requires hybrid support**

Technology alone cannot solve this problem. Participants emphasized the importance of hybrid support models, including mentors, community centers, and schools. Technology should augment in-person support, not replace it.

**5. Maternal health stakeholders need shared definitions and metrics**

For the maternal health challenge, the core issue is data fragmentation across organizations. Multiple stakeholders analyze the same datasets separately. This could results In duplicated effort, inconsistent metrics, and fragmented reporting. The ecosystem lacks a shared understanding of key indicators.

**6. Data clarity is needed for advocacy and funding**

Workshop notes emphasized that clearer shared data would help advocacy organizations, grant applications, strategic planning, and policy alignment. Better coordination would allow stakeholders to focus on action rather than spreadsheet creation.

**7. Technology should support experts, not replace them**

Participants stressed that maternal health analysis requires expertise. Technology should simplify data aggregation, standardize metrics, and visualize trends. But not replace expert interpretation.

**PILLAR: THRIVING FAMILIES\
Targeted Problem 1: Expanding Youth Employment Pathways**

**Problem Statement**

How might we use technology to improve how Richmond youth find, prepare for, and access early employment opportunities---including summer jobs, internships, and job-shadowing experiences---so that the path from interest to employment feels clear, supported, and achievable, while respecting youth labor laws, parental consent requirements, staffing capacity, and transportation barriers?

**Context**

Early work experiences help young people build skills, confidence, and long-term workforce attachment. For many Richmond youth---especially those without strong adult support networks---these opportunities provide critical exposure to career pathways and financial independence. However, the process of securing a first job is often fragmented and confusing. Youth may struggle to:

- find appropriate job opportunities

- organize required documents

- understand labor law requirements

- prepare resumes or applications

- navigate transportation limitations

Existing programs through the Office of Community Wealth Building and community organizations provide support, but access is not always scalable or easy to navigate. Employers also face barriers in hiring youth, including uncertainty around labor rules and a lack of streamlined ways to connect with prepared candidates.

The opportunity is to create a system that helps youth move from interest to preparation, and employment, while also helping employers feel confident hiring youth.

**Solutions must:**

- respect state youth labor laws and parental consent requirements

- prioritize privacy protections for minors

- avoid adding significant workload for City staff

- recognize transportation limitations affecting youth job access

- complement existing workforce and community programs rather than replace them

**Success Would Mean**

- Youth have one clear place to explore employment opportunities and career experiences.

- Teens receive guidance on preparing for their first job.

- Required documents can be organized digitally and securely.

- Employers feel confident hiring youth candidates.

- More Richmond youth successfully access early employment opportunities.

**PILLAR: THRIVING FAMILIES\
Targeted Problem 2: Inclusion of "People Data" on Open Data Portal**

**Problem Statement**

How might we use technology to create a repository for demographic, education, health, and other "people" data on the City of Richmond's Open Data portal. Currently, the Open Data Portal has several data sources, primarily focused on governmental services, emergency response, and various mapping datasets. While some of the categories on the site -- including "Unique, Health, and Inclusive Neighborhoods" and "Education and Workforce Development" -- have labels that sound people focused, the data sets are limited to mapping and geographic landmarks.

**Context**

Human service, education, and health organizations across Richmond---including City agencies, nonprofit partners, and others---rely on multiple public datasets such as:

- **[Census and ACS population data](https://data.census.gov/)**

- **[Virginia Department of Health](https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/data/)**

- [**Virginia Department of Education**](https://www.doe.virginia.gov/data-policy-funding/data-reports)

- [**Kids Count data**](https://vakids.org/kids-count)

- **[Virginia Department of Medical Assistance Services](https://www.dmas.virginia.gov/data-reporting/)**

- **[Virginia Department of Social Services](https://www.dss.virginia.gov/geninfo/reports/)**

Currently, organizations independently compile and analyze these datasets, resulting in duplicated effort, inconsistent metrics, and fragmented reporting. Data needs to be available at the local level and state level, for comparison. Data should also be able to be disaggregated by age level (e.g., 0-5, school age, 18-24, older adults, etc.), and other characteristics (e.g., insurance coverage, race/ethnicity).

Creating a shared "people" data space on the existing Open Data Portal would allow for residents, community partners, nonprofits, and public agencies to have a trusted and credible source for some of the most common demographic and human service data indicators.

**Solutions must:**

- operate within legal and policy limits around sharing data

- respect limited staffing capacity across agencies and nonprofits

- remain cost-effective and sustainable

**Success Would Mean**

- The City of Richmond has "people data" easily accessible on the City's Open Data Portal

- There is a mechanism for consistent and timely updates to datasets

- There is a process for people using the data to submit questions and provide feedback on the datasets included in the Open Data Portal

- Organizations no longer recreate the same datasets independently.

- Grant applications use shared and standardized data.

- Staff time shifts from manual data compilation to strategic analysis.

- Funding and interventions are directed toward evidence-based priorities.
